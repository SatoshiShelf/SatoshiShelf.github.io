The checklist

Six signs it's genuinely Bitcoin-only

1. A plain address or QR — not a hosted page

If checkout sends you to a processor's branded page asking for an email, it isn't Bitcoin-only. It's a payment gateway wearing a Bitcoin hat.

2. No card fallback

The moment there's a "pay by card" button next to it, the Bitcoin option is a marketing surface, not the mechanism.

3. No account required

A store that needs a login has a customer database. A Bitcoin-only shop can hand you the file without knowing who you are.

4. Real settlement, not IOUs

On-chain payments can be checked on a public block explorer. Nothing is held in an internal store balance you must trust.

5. No email harvest

Instant delivery means there's no reason to ask for an address. A mandatory email field is a marketing list, not a delivery mechanism.

6. The price is in sats or a live rate

It shows you exactly what to send, converted at a rate you can check — not a vague "contact us for a quote".

Also worth knowing

Other places to spend Bitcoin on digital goods

The Bitcoin-only corner of the web is smaller than it should be, and that's a feature — it's mostly run by individuals rather than growth teams. When you're evaluating any of them, apply the six checks above: a real Bitcoin checkout, no card fallback, no account, actual settlement, no email requirement, transparent pricing. A store that passes all six is almost always a real one operated by someone who actually uses Bitcoin.

We'd rather you buy from a shop that treats Bitcoin as money than from a shop that treats it as a marketing channel — even when that shop isn't ours.
