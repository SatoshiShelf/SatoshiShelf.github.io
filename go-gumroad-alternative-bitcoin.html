Gumroad is a good default for creators who want cards, tax handling, and a storefront in an afternoon. A self-hosted Bitcoin store is a different bargain: more control, more setup, no platform cut, and no built-in discovery.

This is not a “Gumroad is bad” post. Gumroad is a mature platform with payments, tax handling, and a marketplace. The question is whether its bargain fits what you sell and who buys it.

The 30-second answer

Choose Gumroad if you want card payments, sales-tax handling, and a store live today, and you are comfortable with its fees and content rules.

Choose a self-hosted Bitcoin store if your buyers are Bitcoin-native or card-constrained, you want direct settlement to your wallet, you do not want a platform cut, and you can handle checkout, delivery, support, and tax yourself.

What Gumroad does better

  • Cards and familiarity. Most buyers already know how to pay with a card.
  • Tax handling. As merchant of record, Gumroad removes a large operational burden for many creators.
  • Discovery. The marketplace can send buyers you would not otherwise reach.
  • Speed. You can list a product without touching a server, a wallet, or a verification endpoint.

If those advantages matter more than the fee or the control, Gumroad is the rational choice. “Build everything yourself” is not a personality trait.

What self-hosting does better

  • No platform cut. The sale settles to your wallet; there is no 10% line item.
  • No account gate. A buyer can pay from any Bitcoin wallet without creating a store account.
  • No chargebacks. Confirmed on-chain payments are final.
  • Your customer relationship. You decide what data to collect, and you can choose to collect none.
  • Your rules. No marketplace can delist a product or change the checkout copy without your involvement.

The hidden work

The storefront is the visible part. The work is underneath:

  • Generating and tracking receiving addresses.
  • Quoting BTC from a fiat price and locking the rate.
  • Verifying transactions and preventing txid replay.
  • Issuing expiring download tokens.
  • Handling underpayments, late payments, and support.
  • Understanding sales tax, refund rules, and record-keeping in your jurisdiction.
  • Bringing your own traffic, because there is no marketplace feed.

If that list sounds like a weekend project, it can be. If it sounds like a second job, use a platform or a hosted gateway.

Migration checklist

  1. Export the product list. Names, descriptions, prices, file names, and preview assets.
  2. Pick the first product. Migrate one product with a simple delivery path before touching the catalog.
  3. Rebuild the page on your domain. Keep the product promise and the price; improve the clarity.
  4. Set up the wallet and verification. Test the full flow with a real payment, not just a testnet transaction.
  5. Write the policies. Refunds, delivery, support, and data collection.
  6. Redirect what you can. Keep old links alive if the platform allows it; otherwise update your bios and pinned posts.
  7. Announce the move. Tell existing buyers where to find the product and how support works.

You can run both. A hybrid store is often the honest answer: Gumroad for card buyers and discovery, a direct Bitcoin store for buyers who want it. Keep the two checkouts clearly separate.

FAQ

Does Gumroad accept Bitcoin?
Gumroad’s checkout is built around cards and its own payout system. It is not a direct Bitcoin checkout where the buyer pays your wallet. If direct Bitcoin payment is the requirement, you need a different checkout path.
Is a self-hosted store really free?
There is no platform percentage, but it is not free. You pay for hosting, a domain if you want one, and the time to build and maintain checkout, verification, delivery, and support. For a small catalog, those costs can still be low.
Who handles sales tax?
Gumroad acts as merchant of record and handles sales tax collection and remittance for sales on its platform. With a direct store, that responsibility is generally yours. This is one of the biggest practical differences and should be part of the decision.
Can I keep Gumroad and add a Bitcoin store?
Yes. Keep Gumroad for card buyers and run a separate direct store for Bitcoin buyers. Use distinct product URLs or a clear checkout choice. Do not pretend the two checkout flows are the same thing.
What about chargebacks?
Card payments can be disputed. On-chain Bitcoin payments cannot be charged back through the card network. A direct store still needs a refund policy and a way to handle genuine mistakes.
How hard is migration?
The files are easy to move; the customer relationship and the tax process are the hard parts. Export your product list, rewrite the pages in your own design, test delivery, and keep a redirect from the old store where possible.
